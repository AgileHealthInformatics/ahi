# Health Data Operability Maturity Assessment: Workshop Facilitation Guide

[Open the interactive facilitation guide](health_data_operability_workshop_facilitation_guide.html)

## About this resource

The **Workshop Facilitation Guide** supports facilitated use of the Health Data Operability Maturity Assessment.

It is designed for a multidisciplinary workshop in which participants assess current capability, examine evidence, discuss disagreement and agree practical next steps.

The default agenda is suitable for a **half-day workshop**, although facilitators can adapt it to the context.

## Core workshop principles

The guide emphasises:

- evidence over aspiration;
- capability over technology;
- cumulative maturity;
- real examples over impressions;
- navigation rather than judgement;
- recognition that maturity profiles can be uneven;
- separation of documented practice from demonstrated practice; and
- use of **Not known** where evidence is insufficient.

## Preparing the workshop

Before the session:

1. define the assessment purpose and scope;
2. name the priority data clearly;
3. invite appropriate multidisciplinary participants;
4. circulate the six dimensions, maturity levels and response scale;
5. ask participants to bring relevant evidence;
6. appoint a recorder where useful; and
7. open the assessment toolkit ready for use.

## Participants and roles

The guide describes potential participants and separate responsibilities for:

- facilitator;
- assessment sponsor; and
- recorder.

A useful workshop normally includes people who collectively understand data meaning, operations, governance, lifecycle management, quality, architecture and service delivery.

## The six dimensions

The facilitation prompts cover:

- semantic governance;
- context capture;
- process awareness;
- accountability and stewardship;
- lifecycle continuity and preservation; and
- fitness assurance.

## Managing disagreement

Do not force consensus merely to complete the assessment.

Where participants have materially different views:

- identify the point of disagreement;
- ask for concrete examples and evidence;
- record uncertainty or local variation;
- use a lower confidence where appropriate; and
- identify evidence that would resolve the question.

## Reviewing the profile

The group should explicitly review:

- the constraining dimension;
- claimed versus evidenced maturity;
- uneven maturity;
- implications for automated and AI use; and
- anti-pattern flags.

## Agreeing next steps

Aim to identify a manageable set of priority actions, normally three to six where possible.

Record action owners and a trigger or timing for review.

## Interactive features

The guide includes:

- **Jump to section** navigation;
- **Expand all prompts**;
- **Collapse all prompts**;
- **Print guide**; and
- **Print checklist**.

The printable checklist can be used before, during and at the close of the session.

## Follow-up

After the workshop, retain the exported assessment, report and evidence references in accordance with organisational policy.

The assessment should be revisited when material changes occur or when the agreed review point is reached.

## Technical notes

The interactive HTML resource is self-contained and intended for static hosting. It uses standard HTML, embedded CSS and vanilla JavaScript, so no application server or build process is required.
