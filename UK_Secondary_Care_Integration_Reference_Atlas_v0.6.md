# UK Secondary Care Integration Reference Atlas

[Open the interactive atlas](UK_Secondary_Care_Integration_Reference_Atlas_v0.6.html)

## About this resource

The **UK Secondary Care Integration Reference Atlas** is a capability-led comparison of secondary-care integration and data flows across **England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland**.

It is designed as a practical reference for comparing how broadly similar secondary-care capabilities are supported by different national architectures, services and information flows. It also includes a view of alignment with the emerging **European Health Data Space (EHDS)** architecture.

The atlas is a comparison and navigation aid rather than a specification for a single UK-wide architecture.

## What it helps you do

Use the atlas to:

- compare national integration patterns across the four UK nations;
- examine similarities and differences in secondary-care data flows;
- distinguish established capability from transition, mixed arrangements, gaps or areas that remain unclear;
- explore implications for cross-border care;
- examine architectural alignment with EHDS;
- filter the capability comparison by domain and status;
- inspect individual data-flow descriptions; and
- review the source register and stated limitations behind the comparison.

## How to use the atlas

### 1. Start with the executive judgement

The opening section summarises the overall comparison and highlights the principal architectural differences between the four nations.

### 2. Review the national integration patterns

The atlas characterises the four national patterns before moving into detailed comparison. This is useful context because similar capabilities can be implemented through materially different national arrangements.

### 3. Examine EHDS alignment

The EHDS section considers alignment under themes including:

- primary use and cross-border care;
- interoperable EHR systems;
- secondary use; and
- overall architectural alignment.

The assessments are comparative indicators, not formal EHDS conformity determinations.

### 4. Use the capability comparison matrix

The comparison matrix can be filtered using:

- **Search**;
- **Domain**; and
- **Status contains**.

Status values include established, transition, mixed, gap and unclear.

Use the search box for capability terms such as referral or medication.

### 5. Explore data flows

The **Data-flow explorer** provides a more detailed view of individual capabilities and their national implementations.

Selecting an item reveals the relevant flow and supporting comparison information.

### 6. Review cross-border implications

The cross-border section draws together the implications of nationally scoped identity, differing infrastructure patterns and the need for a common semantic layer.

### 7. Download the comparison data

Use **Download comparison CSV** when you want a portable tabular copy of the comparison for further review or analysis.

## Interpretation

The atlas compares national arrangements at a capability and architecture level. It should not be read as evidence that every local provider within a nation implements a capability identically.

Where the page marks an area as mixed, in transition, a gap or unclear, that classification should be read in the context of the evidence described in the atlas rather than as proof of absence.

The source register should be consulted when a comparison is being used to support a material architectural or procurement decision.
