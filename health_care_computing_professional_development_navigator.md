# Health & Care Computing Professional Development Navigator

## About this resource

The **Health & Care Computing Professional Development Navigator** is an interactive, self-directed professional development guide produced by **Agile Health Informatics Ltd**.

It is designed to help people working in, or alongside, health and care informatics understand the breadth of the field, identify areas relevant to their role or interests, explore related capabilities, and build a personalised development plan.

The navigator is **not** a certification programme, formal curriculum, competency award or accredited course. It does not assume that every user needs the same depth of knowledge in every area. Instead, it provides a structured way to explore the field and decide what to learn next.

The page is deliberately vendor-neutral. Standards, methods and learning resources are included to support understanding and professional development rather than to promote particular products.

## Who it is for

The navigator is intended for a broad health and care audience, including:

- clinicians and managers taking on digital or informatics responsibilities;
- enterprise, solution and data architects;
- analysts, data professionals and engineers;
- interoperability and integration specialists;
- information governance, cyber security and clinical safety practitioners;
- analytics, research and population health professionals;
- operational and service-management staff;
- people moving into health informatics from another discipline; and
- experienced professionals who want to broaden into adjacent areas.

No prior computer-science education is assumed.

## What the navigator contains

The guide organises health and care computing into **13 connected capability domains**:

1. Foundations of health and care informatics
2. Clinical terminology, coding and semantic standards
3. Information governance, ethics and data protection
4. Interoperability and technical standards
5. Enterprise and solution architecture
6. Data management, modelling and quality
7. Analytics, business intelligence and population health management
8. Clinical safety and human factors
9. Cyber security and information assurance
10. Artificial intelligence and clinical decision support
11. Workflow, process and pathway modelling
12. Operational, infrastructure and service management disciplines
13. Specialist clinical and operational informatics domains

The ordering is not a hierarchy. Most professional roles draw on several domains at once.

The page also identifies:

- specialist informatics areas;
- cross-cutting professional themes;
- emerging capability gaps;
- learning and reference resources; and
- relationships between adjacent disciplines.

## How to use the navigator

### 1. Choose your current level

Start in the **Navigator** section and select the level that best reflects the depth you currently need:

- **Foundation** for building awareness and a working vocabulary;
- **Practitioner** for applying concepts in real work; or
- **Specialist / Leadership** for deeper expertise, assurance, design or strategic responsibility.

These levels are indicative rather than rigid. You may be highly experienced in one domain and at foundation level in another.

### 2. Select your areas of interest

Choose one or more areas that reflect your current role, responsibilities or development goals.

The navigator uses these choices to change the emphasis and ordering of the capability domains. It does not remove the rest of the field. This is intentional, because important dependencies and adjacent disciplines can otherwise be overlooked.

### 3. Review your personalised route

The page generates a suggested route through the capability domains.

Domains are prioritised as:

- **Core**: directly relevant to your selected interests;
- **Develop**: useful adjacent capabilities that are likely to strengthen your practice; and
- **Explore**: areas that remain part of the wider professional landscape.

Use this as a guide rather than as a mandatory sequence.

### 4. Explore individual capability domains

Each domain includes a concise explanation of:

- what the domain covers;
- why it matters;
- important topics and concepts;
- typical professional roles;
- adjacent disciplines; and
- guidance appropriate to the selected experience level.

Open the detailed information for domains that are particularly relevant to you.

The search facility can also be used to find concepts such as **FHIR**, **clinical safety**, **OMOP**, **workflow**, **terminology**, **architecture** or **AI**.

### 5. Use the capability map

The **Capability Map** provides another way to explore the 13 domains.

It is useful when you want to understand the field as a connected whole rather than follow a personalised route. Selecting a domain reveals its relationship to the wider professional landscape.

### 6. Review specialist informatics areas

The **Specialisms** section highlights areas in which general informatics capabilities meet specialist clinical or operational practice.

These include fields such as imaging, pathology, pharmacy, records and document management, genomics, research informatics and clinical decision support.

They are presented as connected specialisms rather than isolated professional silos.

### 7. Consider the cross-cutting themes

The **Themes** section identifies concerns that apply across multiple domains, including:

- data quality and provenance;
- governance and accountability;
- interoperability;
- clinical safety;
- human factors and usability;
- socio-technical systems thinking;
- ethics, trust and consent;
- equity and inclusion; and
- operational resilience.

These should be considered alongside subject-specific knowledge.

### 8. Review emerging areas

The navigator also identifies areas where workforce capability is still developing or where strategic ambition may be ahead of broadly distributed professional experience.

These are useful for identifying longer-term development priorities and areas worth monitoring.

## Creating a personal development plan

The **Study plan** section of the navigation opens the personalised development-plan builder.

Although the navigation label uses “Study plan”, the generated output is intended as a **self-directed professional development plan**, not a formal programme of study.

### Configure the plan

You can specify:

- your name, role or plan owner;
- a personalised plan title;
- the duration of the plan;
- the number of development hours available each week;
- an optional start date; and
- the capability domains you want to include.

The current experience level is taken from the main Navigator.

### Use the navigator recommendations

Select **Use navigator recommendations** to populate the plan with domains suggested from your current level and areas of interest.

You can then add or remove domains manually before creating the plan.

### Generate the plan

Select **Generate development plan**.

The navigator produces a week-by-week plan containing:

- a weekly capability focus;
- a suggested objective;
- relevant topics;
- an indicative allocation of time between reading, applied learning and reflection;
- suggested reference organisations or resources;
- related disciplines;
- space for notes and evidence; and
- an end-of-plan reflection.

If the selected duration is longer than the number of chosen domains, important domains may be revisited in later phases so that learning can move from orientation to application and integration.

## Printing or saving the plan

Use **Print development plan** after generating the plan.

The print view removes the rest of the website and presents only the development plan in a print-friendly format.

From the browser print dialogue you can:

- print a paper copy; or
- choose **Save as PDF** to retain an electronic copy.

The printed plan includes space for completion marking, notes and evidence.

## Learning resources

The **Resources** section suggests organisations, standards bodies and learning sources relevant to the route you have selected.

These are **illustrative rather than exhaustive**. Course availability, URLs and provider offerings can change, so current information should be checked before committing to a particular programme.

The resource list is intended to help users find credible starting points, not to prescribe a single provider or learning pathway.

## Saving preferences

The navigator stores selected development-plan settings locally in the browser where supported.

This allows you to return to the page on the same device and continue working with your previous configuration.

No account is required by the page itself.

## Light and dark modes

Use the theme control in the page header to switch between light and dark presentation.

The selected theme is retained locally where the browser permits it.

## Important limitations

This resource is intended as a **professional development aid**.

It does not:

- confer certification, accreditation or professional registration;
- demonstrate that a person is competent in a particular discipline;
- replace employer-specific role requirements;
- replace statutory, regulatory or professional obligations;
- prescribe a single career path or learning sequence;
- guarantee that listed external resources remain available; or
- substitute for specialist training where formal competence or authorisation is required.

The development levels are deliberately flexible because professional capability is domain-specific. A person may appropriately operate at different levels across different parts of the navigator.

## Recommended way to use the resource

For most users, a practical sequence is:

1. Select your current experience level.
2. Choose the interests most relevant to your work.
3. Review the suggested Core and Develop domains.
4. Explore adjacent domains before narrowing your priorities.
5. Use the capability map to check that important dependencies have not been overlooked.
6. Select a manageable group of domains for development.
7. Generate a personal development plan.
8. Print or save the plan as a PDF.
9. Record learning, applied work and evidence as you progress.
10. Revisit the navigator periodically as your role or interests change.
