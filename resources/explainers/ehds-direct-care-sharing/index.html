    Standards & architecture
    What is specified, and what is still being defined?
    The Regulation establishes EEHRxF, including harmonised datasets, coding systems and technical specifications. Final technical specifications are due by 26 March 2027. Published content guidance includes Patient Summary Release 3.4, ePrescription/eDispensation Release 3.1, Laboratory Result Release 1.2, Medical Imaging Release 1.1 and Hospital Discharge Report Release 1.1. The standards below distinguish the legal framework from existing implementation foundations.
    

    Legal exchange format
    EEHRxF
    A machine-readable format comprising harmonised datasets, coding systems and values, plus technical interoperability specifications such as content representation, standards and profiles. The Regulation requires data to be issued in the format and receiving providers to accept and be able to read automated exchanges.
    Status: binding framework; detailed technical specifications due by 26 March 2027.
    

    Cross-border service
    MyHealth@EU
    The EU infrastructure links national services. Each country’s National Contact Point for Digital Health acts as an organisational and technical gateway. It carries cross-border services such as Patient Summary and ePrescription/eDispensation, with the scope expanding in stages.
    Status: existing service infrastructure is evolving to support EHDS obligations.
    

    Existing technical foundations
    eHealth Network specifications
    Current cross-border Patient Summary and ePrescription services use established guidelines and technical specifications. Earlier Commission recommendations identify HL7 CDA Release 2 for document exchange, DICOM for imaging, and relevant IHE profiles for functions such as patient identification, document exchange and audit.
    Status: implementation foundations, not a claim that the EHDS final EEHRxF mandates one syntax for every category.
    

    Clinical meaning
    Datasets, terminology and units
    Shared structure alone is insufficient. The exchange needs aligned clinical concepts, value sets, identifiers, units, language handling and provenance so the receiving team can interpret information safely. Exact bindings and profiles are category-specific and must follow the applicable specifications.
    Status: the Regulation requires coding systems and values; this demonstrator does not prescribe a final terminology binding.
    

    System capability
    Interoperability and logging components
    EHDS introduces harmonised EHR-system components for exchanging priority data in EEHRxF and for recording access. These components are intended to support data import/export and reviewable access logs. Product conformity and national operational controls are part of implementation.
    Status: requirements are being operationalised through further specifications.
    

    Not implied
    No single central record
    The exchange model connects care providers and national services. It does not imply a single EU database containing everyone’s complete longitudinal record, nor that every data item is automatically transmitted whenever a person crosses a border.
    Design implication: availability, identity matching, authorisation, context, freshness and safe presentation remain operational responsibilities.
    

    FHIR caution: HL7 FHIR may be relevant to national architectures and future profiles, but the EHDS Regulation does not itself state that all primary-use exchanges must use FHIR. HL7 CDA is part of existing cross-border specifications. The implementing acts and applicable service specifications determine the required exchange artefacts.
    

    Rights & safeguards
    Access is governed, contextual and visible
    The workflow must be read alongside rights for individuals and professional duties for care providers. The demonstrator simplifies these controls for teaching.
    Identify and authenticateEstablish the patient and the health professional using relevant national services.
    Check the care contextAccess is associated with providing healthcare and governed by applicable rules.
    Apply patient controlsAccess restrictions, access logs, correction rights and any nationally available exchange opt-out matter.
    Use safely and record accessPresent context and provenance; retain access logging and clinical responsibility.
    Consent is not a universal switch. EHDS provides rights to restrict access to all or parts of personal electronic health data and allows Member States to offer an opt-out from cross-border exchange. The legal basis and exact access rules are shaped by the Regulation and national implementation. This explainer does not model a single pan-European consent screen or imply that every exchange requires the same consent step.
    Individual controls
    Individuals are to be able to access and download data, view access logs, request rectification, add information, appoint an authorised person and apply access restrictions, with the implementation details supported through national services.
    Clinical interpretation
    Receipt of a structured record does not itself make information clinically complete or correct. The receiving professional must interpret it in context, including source, date, language, uncertainty and whether information is missing or out of date.
    

    Implementation timeline
    Primary-use exchange arrives in stages
    These milestones describe the EHDS timetable published by the European Commission. National readiness and existing MyHealth@EU services may move ahead of the common application dates.
    26 March 2025
    Regulation enters into force
    The transition and preparation period begins.
    26 March 2027
    EEHRxF specifications due
    The Commission deadline for implementing acts setting the technical specifications for the priority categories.
    March 2029
    First categories
    Patient Summaries and ePrescriptions/eDispensations should be exchanged in all Member States.
    March 2031
    Second categories
    Medical images, laboratory results and hospital discharge reports should be operational for exchange in all Member States.
    Capability versus outcome: a legal requirement and a common exchange format create necessary conditions. They do not by themselves prove that a patient’s relevant information will be available, correctly matched, current, understood or acted upon at the point of care.
    

    

    Use and limits
    A demonstrator, not a conformance tool
    This static page uses a synthetic workflow and standards-informed descriptions. It does not connect to MyHealth@EU, verify identities or eligibility, perform clinical coding, exchange real health data, implement national access rules, validate an EEHRxF payload or provide legal advice. Final technical obligations depend on the Regulation, its implementing acts and applicable national and service specifications.
