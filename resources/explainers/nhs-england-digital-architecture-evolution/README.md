# NHS England Digital Architecture Evolution

An interactive, evidence-led explainer of how digital health architecture in England has evolved, which capabilities became durable foundations, and where programme resets and fragmented implementation created persistent problems.

## Suggested website location

`/resources/explainers/nhs-england-digital-architecture-evolution/`

The page is self-contained, using embedded CSS and vanilla JavaScript with no external libraries or image dependencies. The Agile Health Informatics logo is embedded in the HTML. External evidence links require an internet connection; the timeline, architecture-layer explorer and findings register work locally.

## Current coverage

Evidence is reviewed to 17 October 2026. The revised account records approval of the Single Patient Record Full Business Case and the programme’s move into mobilisation, while making clear that key architecture and procurement decisions remain open. It notes the stated 2028 ambition for NHS App access, subject to legislation, and planned maternity and frailty pilots. These are programme milestones and intentions, not evidence of an operational national record.

The source note for *Information for Health* now points to the Wellcome Collection catalogue record for the published strategy, as an official online copy is no longer maintained.

## Page features

- Timeline of strategic and programme shifts from 1998 to the current national architecture work.
- Interactive view across six architecture layers and five periods.
- Evidence-led assessment of durable foundations and persistent coherence problems.
- Filterable findings register, source links and a limitations section.
- Responsive site navigation, theme control using the shared `ahi-theme` preference, and print-friendly layout.

## Evidence and limitations

The explainer is a selective national history of England, not a complete programme audit or a local readiness assessment. Its architecture-layer display is an analytical synthesis, not an authoritative NHS diagram. It distinguishes published programme intent and service evidence from architectural interpretation; programme milestones do not demonstrate consistent adoption or improved outcomes.

Review the cited sources and update the page when programme status, architecture decisions or delivery evidence changes. In particular, revise the Single Patient Record section as procurement, target architecture, legislative dependencies and pilot findings become clearer.
