1

Why a stewardship network

Terminology servers, metadata registries and data catalogues are now affordable and mature. What they hold is only as trustworthy as the people who define and maintain it.

Infrastructure without content

A national terminology or data dictionary platform can be live for years and still hold few definitions that clinicians trust. Platforms store meaning; they cannot decide it.

Expertise sits elsewhere

The people who know what an observation, a pathway or an indicator means work in clinical programmes, registries, laboratories and community services, not in the national data office.

Authority sits elsewhere too

A data office cannot set the priorities of clinicians, regional staff or independent registries. Asking for goodwill produces a voluntary community that fades when people get busy.

The same figure, calculated differently

Without one owner per indicator, two reporting teams can publish different numbers for the same measure. Nobody can explain a published figure back to its source.

Assets lost when funding ends

Research and EU-funded projects build excellent dictionaries and concept sets. When the grant ends, they stop being maintained unless a lasting organisation adopts them.

Rising legal and regulatory demands

Data access and interoperability regulation increasingly requires health services to describe their datasets, their quality and their definitions. That needs someone accountable for each one.

Three bad alternatives. Without a network, a data office can write clinical content centrally (not credible, does not scale), import definitions in bulk with no owners (volume without trust), or wait for demand (services go unused). A stewardship network avoids all three.

2

What stewardship covers

Stewardship is not only about a data dictionary. It covers a chain that lets anyone trace a published figure back to where the data was recorded.

TerminologyWhat codes meanCode systems, value sets and maps, such as SNOMED CT and LOINC, held in a terminology service.
Reference dataWhich values are allowedShared code lists: organisations, service types, specialties, medicinal products.
Data dictionaryWhat data items meanData elements and datasets with definitions, units, value domains and versions.
Reporting metadataHow figures are calculatedIndicators and measures: numerator, denominator, exclusions, derivation, reporting period.
Data asset catalogueWhat data is held, and where it goesSystems, datasets and registers with owners, classification, quality and lineage.

Each link in the chain has a named person responsible for it. A domain steward owns the link between a data element and its value set. A measure steward owns the link between an indicator and the data elements it uses. A data asset owner owns the link between an asset and the datasets it holds.

Keep the parts separate, but link them. Terminology belongs in the terminology service and definitions in the dictionary. Stewardship connects them by identifier rather than copying content from one to the other.

Design principles

Ten principles that keep the network credible, scalable and sustainable.

PrincipleWhat it means in practice
Expertise stays where it isThe central team coordinates and assures. It does not try to become the clinical authority for every domain.
Authority follows accountabilityEach group of stewards is mandated through its own line: clinical leadership, operations, or a written agreement for bodies outside the health service.
Stewardship is separate from approvalStewards draft and recommend. Designated boards approve national standards. Taking part in a project does not confer authority over them.
Lasting organisations sponsorDomains are sponsored by bodies with ongoing remits. Time-limited projects contribute through them.
Reuse before writing anything newEvery domain starts by finding and assessing what already exists.
Match the level of control to the assetOnly national standards go to a board. Domain and project assets follow lighter routes.
Participation is formal and resourcedNamed roles, protected time and written agreements.
Give contributors something backFaster turnaround, published and versioned assets, less local mapping work, recognition.
No domain is left outCommunity, primary care, mental health and disability services help set priorities, not just review others' work.
Trace every figure to its sourceIndicators, definitions, value sets and data assets are linked, and stewardship covers the links.
4

How to set it up

No single function in a national health service controls everyone the network needs. Split accountability along existing management lines and bring it together under one executive mandate.

Leadership and accountability

LevelTypicallyAccountable forDoes not
Executive sponsorChief executive and executive teamMandate, allocating accountability, settling disputes between lines, reviewing progressRun the network or decide content
Clinical co-leadChief clinical, medical or nursing officerClinical meaning, sponsoring clinical domains, nominating stewards, clinical authorityOperate platforms or the approval workflow
Data and services co-leadChief data officer or national data officeTerminology, reference data and metadata services, process, technical assurance, the coordination teamDecide clinical meaning
Operational leadRegional or operational directorsReleasing local custodians, implementation evidence, adoptionApprove national content
External partnersRegulators, registries, independent providers, universitiesWhat their written agreements commit them toTake instruction from health service management

These meet in a stewardship council, co-chaired by the clinical and data co-leads. It sets priorities, admits new domains, owns the charter and reports to the executive team. It does not approve content.

Four tiers

Executive

Chief executive and executive team. Mandate down, reporting up.

1. National governance

Stewardship council; a data definitions board and a terminology editorial board (or one board with two panels); national standards bodies. Recommendations go up, approvals come down.

2. Coordination hub

A small central team hosted by the data office with a clinical lead. Charter, participation register, intake, onboarding, tooling, quality checks, reporting.

3. Domain groups

One per domain, for example critical care, medicines, reporting and audit, community care, genomics. Sponsor, lead steward, domain stewards, clinical authority.

4. Local implementation

Regions, hospitals, registries and system programmes. Local custodians show how definitions are actually recorded and whether they can be implemented.

How content moves

  1. Propose. A steward submits a new item or a change, with its source and licence.
  2. Triage. The hub checks scope and duplicates, assigns an asset tier and sets a target turnaround.
  3. Author. The steward builds the content, with hub support. AI-drafted content is marked and reviewed by a named person.
  4. Validate. Technical checks by the hub; clinical review by the clinical authority.
  5. Test locally. At least one local custodian confirms it can be implemented.
  6. Consult. Affected domains and registered users can comment.
  7. Approve. The board decides and the decision is recorded.
  8. Publish. A versioned release through the services' interfaces, with release notes.
  9. Maintain. Scheduled review, changes, supersession or retirement. Nothing is deleted.

Three asset tiers

A · National

A national standard users are expected to follow. Full workflow and board approval.

B · Domain-recognised

Authoritative within a domain, versioned and published for reuse. Steward and clinical sign-off; the boards are informed.

C · Registered

Has a named steward and can be found, but is not endorsed. For project dictionaries, research concept sets and legacy code lists.

Roadmap

A staged proposal: mobilisation and expansion over approximately 24 months, followed by ongoing operation. Each phase has explicit exit criteria. Durations are indicative.

Phase 0 · months 0–4

Mobilise

  • Executive mandate naming the co-leads
  • Stewardship council and coordination hub set up
  • Charter drafted and endorsed
  • Participation register: existing assets and who maintains them
  • Minimum metadata standard for assets and indicators
  • Two or three pilot sponsors secured
Exit: mandate recorded, charter endorsed, signed agreements, named lead stewards.
Phase 1 · months 4–12

Pilot

  • Two or three domain groups working
  • 10 to 15 artefacts per domain through the full workflow
  • A local custodian in each pilot
  • National indicators registered for one reporting domain
  • Catalogue seeded with the pilot domains' assets
Exit: every workflow step tested, one change processed, effort and turnaround measured.
Phase 2 · months 12–24

Expand

  • Further domains added in waves
  • Charter and workflow revised from pilot lessons
  • All national indicators registered
  • All in-scope datasets catalogued with owners
  • Steward training, forum and recognition
Exit: recurrent funding for steward time; quarterly report to the executive team.
Phase 3 · month 24 on

Sustain

  • Stewardship part of normal business
  • Dataset descriptions produced from the catalogue
  • Procurement contracts reference stewarded content
  • Technical metadata harvested automatically
  • Formal review after three years
Exit: every national element, indicator and dataset has a named steward or owner.

Choosing pilot domains

Good first domains combine a motivated clinical community, existing assets to reuse, and a clear operational need. Typical candidates are critical care (strong research assets), medicines (direct dependency on terminology and reference data), and national reporting and audit (mature dictionaries and indicators). Make sure community and longitudinal care is represented from the start, even if it is not a first pilot, so that hospital and research data do not shape the whole network.

The first 100 days

  1. Seek the mandate. A joint paper from the clinical and data co-leads to the executive team.
  2. Set up the council and name the coordination lead and clinical lead.
  3. Align with existing governance. Build on data standards and specification processes that already exist rather than creating a parallel structure.
  4. Start the participation register. Clinical programmes, registries, professional bodies, regional data leads, research consortia.
  5. Interview pilot sponsors. For each existing asset, record what exists, who maintains it, its licence and its users.
  6. Draft the charter. Roles, accountability, approval routes, contribution rights, maintenance duties, licensing, exit arrangements.
  7. Agree participation templates with HR and legal: one for internal services, one for external bodies.
  8. Set up minimum tooling. An intake form, steward workspaces and an issue log. Check whether existing platforms can hold indicators and the catalogue.
  9. Define baseline measures and start collecting them.
  10. Hold the Phase 0 review and report the outcome to the executive team.
