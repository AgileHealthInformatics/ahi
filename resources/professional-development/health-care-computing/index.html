Purpose and context

What this guide is for

A professional map, not a course catalogue

Modern health and care depends on computing, information, governance, architectural and operational disciplines that many professionals were never formally trained in. The guide provides a coherent map so that people can navigate the field, identify adjacent capabilities and plan their own development.

No prior computer-science education is assumed. The guide is intended for clinicians and managers acquiring digital responsibilities, architects, analysts, engineers and operations staff broadening their range, governance and safety practitioners developing adjacent knowledge, and anyone using it to structure self-directed professional development.

The framing is deliberately vendor-neutral. Named standards, methods and resources are included to support conceptual understanding and applied capability rather than product-specific knowledge.

Professional context

Health and care computing has broadened beyond the deployment of individual electronic patient record systems. Interoperability, shared data, secondary use, AI assurance, digital safety, resilience and cross-organisational architecture now place wider demands on the workforce.

The Quintuple Aim offers a useful test of relevance: population health, experience of care, efficiency, workforce wellbeing and equity all depend materially on the quality, interoperability and ethical use of information.

FEDIP, SFIA and related occupational frameworks can provide external professional context, but this navigator is not a registration or accreditation scheme.

Scope note: this is an interactive professional development guide, not a statutory competency standard, regulatory instrument or substitute for role-specific professional requirements.
