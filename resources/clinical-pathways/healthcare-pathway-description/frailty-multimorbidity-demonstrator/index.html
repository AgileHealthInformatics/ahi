Why standardise clinical pathways?

The problem is not that clinicians lack pathways. It is that pathway meaning is often scattered across guidance, local procedures, EPR configuration, referral forms and tacit knowledge, making cross-organisational care hard to compare and govern.

1

Different teams describe the same pathway differently

Responsibilities, information needs and escalation may be expressed inconsistently or remain implicit.

2

The pathway can become tied to today's systems

When care meaning is embedded in an EPR or local workflow, changing technology can unintentionally change the model of care.

3

Cross-organisational care is difficult to see as one whole

Frailty care can cross primary care, community services, social care, pharmacy, urgent response, virtual wards and hospitals.

The HPDS proposition: represent the intended model of care as a governed, technology-neutral pathway definition, then describe local organisational and technical realisation separately in a pathway implementation.

A simple way to think about HPDS

1. Pathway catalogue

A governed collection where definitions can be found, compared and maintained.

→
2. Pathway definition

The intended model of care and the primary business artefact.

→
3. Pathway implementation

How particular organisations, services and technology realise the definition.

→
4. Pathway instance

The actual course of care for an individual, held outside the HPDS definition.

Guided tour

Five minutes through a recognisable frailty scenario.

The example uses Deterioration identified at home or usual residence to show what the standard adds without requiring the reader to understand the technical model first.

Why a standard helps

Structure should reduce ambiguity without turning the pathway into a software specification.

WD 0.5 keeps business meaning primary. Greater formality is available where needed, but a higher conformance level is not inherently better.

Without a common representation

  • Pathways are difficult to compare across services.
  • Responsibilities and information dependencies may be implicit.
  • Local system design can be confused with clinical intent.
  • Evidence, authority and lifecycle can be conflated.
  • Changing technology may require rediscovering the care model.

With a governed pathway definition

  • Identity, version, lifecycle, authority and intended use are explicit.
  • Population, entry, exit, activities, decisions, roles, outcomes and evidence can be inspected consistently.
  • Local delivery stays in the implementation layer.
  • Relationships can be represented progressively, from Level 1 catalogue links to a complete Level 2 graph.
  • Evidence provenance remains separate from clinical approval.

Reference vs local delivery

One definition can have different local implementations.

Named provider organisations, sites, products, interfaces, local procedures and workflow configuration belong in a PathwayImplementation rather than in the care-model content of the pathway definition.

Information dependencies

A shared record can support the pathway without becoming the pathway.

HPDS describes technology-neutral information requirements, products, constraints and interoperability dependencies. A shared care record, EPR or integration platform is part of local implementation architecture.

Evidence and authority

Evidence provenance is not clinical approval.

The source catalogue distinguishes material grounded directly in guidance from synthesis and professional inference. WD 0.5 separately records authority status, intended use and lifecycle state.

Why the distinction matters

An evidence description tells you where content came from and how it was derived. It does not make the pathway clinically approved, and technical HPDS conformance would not do so either.

How to use this demonstrator
Clinical and service meaning first, HPDS detail when you need it
Start with the guided tour
The first five steps use a frailty scenario to explain why a governed pathway definition is useful without requiring knowledge of modelling languages or APIs.
Explore a pathway you recognise
The catalogue view keeps the source material in clinical and service language. It also makes clear when a record is a supporting governance process rather than an HPDS pathway definition.
Definition and implementation
WD 0.5 keeps the intended model of care separate from named provider organisations, sites, applications, interfaces, local procedures and workflow configuration. Those local details belong in a pathway implementation.
Technical detail
The technical view shows exactly how the source fields map to WD 0.5, which authoring gaps remain, and what can safely be projected into the normative JSON vocabulary without inventing missing information.
