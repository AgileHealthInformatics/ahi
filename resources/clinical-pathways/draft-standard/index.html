Abstract

Healthcare pathways are usually held as guidance documents, diagrams, spreadsheets, local procedures and system configuration. In those forms they are difficult to discover, compare, govern, specialise and relate to the services, information and technology that realise them. The Healthcare Pathway Description Standard (HPDS) specifies a technology-neutral representation of a healthcare pathway as a governed business artefact that can be held in a pathway catalogue.

HPDS defines a conceptual model for pathway definitions, pathway implementations and pathway catalogues; requirements for identification, governance, provenance, lifecycle, authority and intended use; explicit structural, behavioural, dependency and responsibility relationships with descriptive, analysable semantics; semantic binding; specialisation and deviation; references to formal representations expressed in BPMN, CMMN, DMN, HL7 FHIR and similar standards; four graded conformance levels with validation rules; and normative JSON and human-readable catalogue representations. It keeps the intended model of care separate from any formal model, local implementation and individual patient record, and it keeps technical conformance separate from clinical authority, intended use and lifecycle status.

Working Draft 0.5 is a consistency and implementability revision of Working Draft 0.4. It resolves eleven review findings about behavioural semantics, graph validation, named organisations, missing model structures, immutability, Level 4 coverage, formal representations, extensions, adoption burden, implementation readiness and cross-document consistency. It does not widen the scope of the standard. Appendix L lists the changes, Appendix M traces revised requirements to the model and validation rules, and Appendix N lists unresolved issues and missing implementation evidence.

          1. Introduction

          1.1 Purpose

          This section is non-normative.

          Healthcare organisations routinely maintain catalogues of applications, interfaces, services, data assets, standards and capabilities. Pathways themselves are commonly held in less structured forms, including guidance documents, diagrams, spreadsheets, local operating procedures and system configuration. Pathways have been the subject of sustained definitional work [KINSMAN2010], but that work did not produce a representation of the pathway as governed information. As a result, pathways are difficult to discover, compare, govern, specialise, analyse and relate consistently to services, information requirements and technology.

          This document specifies a technology-neutral business representation of a healthcare pathway suitable for maintenance in a pathway catalogue. The representation is designed to remain intelligible to clinicians, managers and architects while supporting structured machine processing. ISO/TC 215, Health informatics, is the committee within whose scope the subject matter most naturally falls; this document is prepared in the conventions of HL7 specifications to support review by health informatics standards communities.

          1.2 Scope

          This document specifies requirements for representing healthcare pathways as technology-neutral, governed business artefacts suitable for inclusion in healthcare pathway catalogues. It specifies:

          1. a conceptual model for pathway definitions, pathway implementations and pathway catalogues, comprising entities, attributes, structured data types, relationships, cardinalities and constraints;
          2. requirements for catalogue identification, discovery, governance, provenance, rights and lifecycle, including the recording of governance events after publication;
          3. representation of population, entry, exit, stages, activities, decisions, events, roles, services, care settings, information requirements, information products, outcomes, measures, constraints, exceptions and evidence;
          4. explicit structural, behavioural, dependency and responsibility relationships, including inclusion of sub-pathways and relationships between pathways, and the descriptive behavioural semantics of those relationships (Chapter 9);
          5. semantic binding to external terminologies, data specifications and information standards;
          6. derivation of technology-neutral interoperability requirements from pathway dependencies;
          7. specialisation of reference pathways and explicit recording of deviations;
          8. authority status and intended use;
          9. reference from a pathway definition to formal representations of it expressed in external process, case, decision or plan-definition standards, and the handling of discrepancies between them;
          10. graded conformance levels, validation rules and conformance claims;
          11. a normative JSON serialisation and a normative human-readable catalogue record.

          Its provisions apply across primary, community, secondary, tertiary and specialist care, diagnostics, mental health, maternity, medicines, rehabilitation, prevention and screening, urgent and emergency care, elective care, long-term condition management, end-of-life care and interfaces with social care. They apply to pathways whose behaviour is largely sequential and to pathways whose behaviour is largely adaptive.

          This document defines descriptive behavioural semantics: a declarative graph that states the intended order, concurrency, choice, repetition, waiting, interruption, escalation and transition between the behavioural nodes of a pathway, and the containment of those nodes in stages. The graph has enough meaning to support the analyses listed in 9.8: structural validation, comparison of pathways and variants, dependency and impact analysis, derivation of interoperability requirements and mapping to formal representations. It does not define execution semantics. Where operational precision is required, it is provided by a formal representation (8.10) or an implementation artefact (8.28.2) expressed in an external standard.

          The following are outside the scope of this document:

          1. determination of clinical content or clinical appropriateness;
          2. representation of individual patient records or pathway instances, other than the reference points recommended in 8.29;
          3. design of messages, application programming interfaces, endpoints, transport protocols or integration products;
          4. execution semantics, including run-time instance state, token or case-state models, scheduling, work assignment, transaction and error handling, evaluation order of expressions, and the behaviour of workflow engines, clinical decision support systems or orchestration platforms;
          5. the definition of computable process, case or decision models, and methods and styles for producing them;
          6. prescription of a particular process notation, information standard, terminology or enterprise architecture tool;
          7. description of processes whose subject is a service or organisation rather than a subject of care, such as governance, audit or quality-improvement processes.
          Note

          A pathway catalogue publisher can maintain descriptions of governance processes alongside a catalogue, but they are not pathway definitions and are not covered by a conformance claim made under this document (7.1).

          1.3 Audience

          This section is non-normative.

          This document is intended for those who describe, maintain, catalogue or use healthcare pathways, including clinicians, clinical informaticians, service managers, commissioners, business analysts, enterprise and data architects, integration specialists, knowledge engineers, IT system suppliers, researchers and pathway improvement teams. Chapters 5 to 16 and Appendices A, B and G are addressed to implementers of producers, consumers and catalogues. Chapter 1 and Appendices D to F are addressed to all readers.

          1.4 Definition, implementation, instance and catalogue

          This section is non-normative.

          A pathway definition states the intended model of care and is the primary catalogue artefact specified by this document. A formal representation is a technology-neutral model of all or part of a definition expressed in another formalism. A pathway implementation records how an organisation or health system realises a pathway definition through local services, workforce, applications, interfaces, locations and procedures. A pathway instance is the course of care experienced by an individual subject of care. A pathway catalogue is a governed collection of pathway definitions.

          Pathway instances contain personal health information and are outside the normative scope of this document; 8.29 recommends reference points that allow instance data held elsewhere to be related to pathway definitions and implementations.

          1.5 Relationship with other standards

          This section is non-normative.

          This document does not replace concept systems, clinical terminologies, information models, process notations, decision languages or exchange standards. ISO 13940 provides a conceptual basis for continuity of care. HL7 FHIR can represent shareable plan definitions and implementation artefacts. BPMN, CMMN and DMN provide precise process, case and decision views, and the HL7 Field Guide to Shareable Clinical Pathways [HL7-SCP-FIELD-GUIDE] gives a method and style for producing and localising such models. WHO SMART Guidelines and Digital Adaptation Kits provide structured, software-neutral guidance artefacts.

          This document provides the governed, catalogue-oriented pathway business artefact. That artefact can reference formal representations expressed in those standards (8.10), be mapped to them (Appendices C to F) and be realised through implementation artefacts that use them (8.28.2). It does not depend on any of them. The mappings in Appendices C to F are informative and have not been verified by round-trip testing (Appendix N).

          1.6 Conformance approach

          This section is non-normative.

          Different catalogue purposes require different degrees of formality. Four cumulative conformance levels are defined for pathway definitions: a governed catalogue record at Level 1, complete and determinate structural and behavioural relationships at Level 2, semantic binding at Level 3 and computable expressions for designated attributes under a declared expression profile at Level 4. A higher level is not inherently better. The appropriate level is determined by intended use. Obligations that apply at every level, and those that arise only at higher levels, are listed in 5.7.

          1.7 Authority and intended use

          This section is non-normative.

          Conformance to this document does not establish that the clinical content of a pathway is correct or appropriate. Authority status, intended use and lifecycle state are recorded independently of one another and of technical conformance.

          1.8 Conventions

          1.8.1 Conformance verbs

          This document uses the conformance verbs used in HL7 specifications, always in upper case:

          1. SHALL and SHALL NOT indicate a requirement that is necessary for conformance; CONF-HPDS-001
          2. SHOULD and SHOULD NOT indicate a recommendation; a conformant description or system can depart from it where there is a documented reason;
          3. MAY indicates a permission.

          The words "can" and "cannot" indicate a possibility or capability and do not express conformance requirements. Conformance verbs appear only in normative text. Notes, examples, informative sections and informative appendices contain no requirements.

          1.8.2 Conformance statement identifiers

          Each normative statement containing SHALL or SHALL NOT is labelled with a conformance statement identifier of the form CONF-HPDS-nnn. The identifiers are assigned in document order for each working draft. They are provisional and become persistent from the first version released for formal review. Validation rules (Appendix A) have persistent identifiers of the form Vnn. Appendix M relates conformance statements, model elements and validation rules. CONF-HPDS-002

          1.8.3 Attribute tables

          In the attribute and structure tables of Chapters 7 and 8, the obligation column uses the codes M (mandatory), C (conditional) and O (optional), with the meanings given in 8.1. Multiplicity is shown as minimum..maximum, where * indicates no upper limit. The descriptions in these tables are normative.

          1.8.4 Normative and informative content

          Chapters 2 to 19 are normative except for notes, examples and sections marked as informative. Appendices A, B, G and J are normative. All other appendices are informative.

          3. Terms and definitions

          For the purposes of this document, the following terms and definitions apply. Terms that ISO 13940:2026 also defines, such as subject of care, are used with the meaning given in ISO 13940:2026 unless defined below.

          Note

          3.1 healthcare pathway

          coordinated set of healthcare and care activities, ordered or grouped, intended to achieve defined outcomes (3.30) for a defined population (3.11) or circumstance

          Note 1 to entry: A healthcare pathway can be largely sequential or largely adaptive, with activities selected according to need.

          3.2 pathway definition

          technology-neutral business description of the intended model of care for a healthcare pathway (3.1)

          Note 1 to entry: The pathway definition is the primary business artefact (3.10) held in a pathway catalogue (3.3).

          3.3 pathway catalogue

          governed collection of pathway definitions (3.2) maintained for discovery, comparison, reuse, analysis and lifecycle management

          3.4 catalogue entry

          record within a pathway catalogue (3.3) that identifies one pathway definition (3.2) and version

          3.5 pathway implementation

          description of how an organisation or health system realises a pathway definition (3.2) through particular organisational and technical arrangements

          3.6 pathway instance

          actual course of care experienced by an individual subject of care in relation to a healthcare pathway (3.1)

          3.7 pathway description

          structured representation of a pathway definition (3.2) or pathway implementation (3.5) conforming to this document

          3.8 reference pathway

          pathway definition (3.2) published by a competent authority for reuse or specialisation (3.37)

          3.9 pathway variant

          pathway definition (3.2) derived from a parent pathway definition through specialisation (3.37)

          3.10 business artefact

          governed representation of business knowledge maintained independently of a particular technical implementation

          3.11 population

          set of subjects of care to whom a healthcare pathway (3.1) applies, defined by criteria

          3.12 entry point

          circumstance or route by which a subject of care may enter a healthcare pathway (3.1)

          3.13 entry criterion

          condition required to hold for a subject of care to enter a healthcare pathway (3.1) through an entry point (3.12)

          3.14 exit point

          circumstance or route by which a subject of care completes, transfers from or otherwise leaves a healthcare pathway (3.1)

          3.15 exit criterion

          condition required to hold for a subject of care to leave a healthcare pathway (3.1) through an exit point (3.14)

          3.16 pathway stage

          subdivision of a healthcare pathway (3.1) grouping activities (3.17), decision points (3.20) or events (3.22) at a level meaningful for business understanding

          Note 1 to entry: The elements of a stage can be ordered, or available in any order while the stage is active (8.8).

          3.17 activity

          action intended to be performed as part of a healthcare pathway (3.1)

          Note 1 to entry: An activity does not necessarily correspond to an executable workflow task.

          3.18 formal representation

          model of all or part of a pathway definition (3.2) expressed in an external process, case, decision or plan-definition formalism and maintained as a technology-neutral view of that definition

          Note 1 to entry: A formal representation is distinct from an implementation artefact (3.51), which realises part of a pathway implementation (3.5).

          3.19 sub-pathway

          healthcare pathway (3.1) included by reference within another healthcare pathway

          3.20 decision point

          point in a healthcare pathway (3.1) at which one or more alternative pathway branches (3.21) are selected

          3.21 pathway branch

          alternative route from a decision point (3.20)

          3.22 event

          clinically or operationally relevant occurrence that can affect pathway behaviour

          3.23 behavioural node

          pathway stage (3.16), activity (3.17), decision point (3.20) or event (3.22)

          Note 1 to entry: An event whose external attribute is true has no structural container and is treated in behavioural analysis only as stated in 9.6.

          3.24 service

          healthcare or care service that delivers or supports part of a healthcare pathway (3.1)

          Note 1 to entry: A service is distinct from any named provider organisation.

          3.25 role

          responsibility for activity, decision-making or coordination, defined independently of named individuals

          3.26 care setting

          context within which care occurs

          EXAMPLE Home, primary care, outpatient care, inpatient care, diagnostic facility, virtual care.

          3.27 information requirement

          specification of information needed to support an activity (3.17) or decision point (3.20)

          3.28 information product

          specification of information generated through an activity (3.17), decision point (3.20) or event (3.22)

          3.29 clinical concept

          unit of clinical or care meaning used within a pathway description (3.7) that can be bound to an external terminology or data specification

          3.30 outcome

          intended result of a healthcare pathway (3.1) or of a component of it

          Note 1 to entry: An outcome is distinct from the completion of process steps.

          3.31 measure

          defined measurable property associated with pathway performance or achievement of an outcome (3.30)

          3.32 constraint

          condition restricting pathway behaviour or implementation

          3.33 temporal constraint

          constraint (3.32) on the timing, duration, interval, recurrence or order of pathway elements (3.47)

          3.34 anchor event

          event (3.22) or element (3.47) relative to which a temporal constraint (3.33) is expressed

          3.35 exception

          circumstance under which normal pathway behaviour does not apply, together with the alternative behaviour

          3.36 evidence source

          guidance, legislation, policy, research evidence, consensus statement or other source supporting pathway content

          3.37 specialisation

          derivation of a pathway definition (3.2) from a parent pathway definition while retaining inherited content and recording deviations (3.38)

          3.38 deviation

          recorded difference between a pathway variant (3.9) or pathway implementation (3.5) and the pathway definition (3.2) from which it derives

          3.39 terminology binding

          association between an element (3.47) and an identified, versioned concept, value set or data element in an external semantic resource

          3.40 local term

          concept used within a pathway description (3.7) that is not bound to an external semantic resource and is identified as local

          3.41 interoperability requirement

          technology-neutral specification of an information exchange on which a healthcare pathway (3.1) depends

          3.42 authority status

          declared level of professional or organisational authority applying to a pathway description (3.7)

          3.43 intended use

          declared purpose for which a pathway description (3.7) is intended to be relied upon

          3.44 clinical authority

          body or individual with professional authority to approve the clinical content of a pathway description (3.7)

          3.45 pathway owner

          organisation or role accountable for a pathway description (3.7)

          3.46 pathway steward

          organisation or role responsible for maintaining a pathway description (3.7)

          3.47 element

          instance of any entity of the conceptual model within a pathway description (3.7)

          3.48 structural relationship

          relationship between elements (3.47) expressing containment or inclusion

          3.49 behavioural relationship

          relationship between elements (3.47) expressing order, concurrency, choice, repetition, waiting, interruption, escalation or transition

          3.50 provenance record

          record of the origin, authorship, approval and derivation of a pathway description (3.7) or of an element (3.47) within it

          3.51 implementation artefact

          workflow definition, case model, decision model, decision support rule, configuration or other technical artefact that realises part of a pathway implementation (3.5)

          3.52 domain profile

          set of additional constraints, required bindings and extensions (3.54) applied to this document for a defined domain or use case

          3.53 expression profile

          profile defining the expression language, evaluation context and semantics required for interoperable computable expressions

          3.54 extension

          identified addition to the conceptual model

          Note 1 to entry: A modifying extension changes the interpretation of the element (3.47) that carries it; a non-modifying extension does not.

          3.55 pathway description producer

          software or process that creates or modifies pathway descriptions (3.7)

          3.56 pathway description consumer

          software or process that reads or interprets pathway descriptions (3.7)

          3.57 governance event

          dated record of a change to the governance state of a published pathway description (3.7), such as a lifecycle transition, a change of authority status, an approval or a review outcome

          Note 1 to entry: Governance events are appended; they do not change the content of the version (14.2).

          attribute of a pathway description (3.7) whose value can change after publication, but only by a governance event (3.57)

          6. Conceptual framework

          6.1 Separation of definition, implementation and instance

          Whether a pathway description describes a pathway definition or a pathway implementation is declared by its root element: HealthcarePathway for a definition and PathwayImplementation for an implementation (Appendix B). A pathway description SHALL NOT contain personal health information. CONF-HPDS-017

          A pathway definition SHALL describe the intended model of care without implementation-specific delivery arrangements. Implementation-specific delivery arrangements are: named provider organisations or teams identified as delivering services, activities or decisions; named locations or sites; applications, interfaces and devices identified by product, supplier or instance; worklists and order sets; local procedures; and named individuals in performing or deciding roles. Such information SHALL be recorded in a PathwayImplementation (8.27). CONF-HPDS-018

          The restriction applies to the care-model content of a definition: its Population, entry and exit, stage, activity, decision, branch, event, role, service, care setting, information, outcome, measure, constraint, exception and interoperability elements and their relationships. It does not apply to governance and provenance metadata, in which organisations and people MAY be named. These are: owner, steward, clinicalAuthority, approvals, rights, governance events, ProvenanceRecord and ChangeRecord authors, EvidenceSource citations, and the publisher and governanceAuthority of a PathwayCatalogue.

          Note

          A definition does not fail conformance because its owner, steward, approver or clinical authority is a named organisation.

          The same separation applies to formal representations. A formal representation referenced by a pathway definition (8.10) SHALL contain no implementation-specific delivery arrangements, in the sense defined above, although its own governance and provenance metadata MAY name organisations and people. A model that has been localised to particular organisations, systems or procedures is an implementation artefact (8.28.2) and SHALL be recorded against a pathway implementation. CONF-HPDS-019

          6.2 Design principles

          The requirements of this document are derived from the principles in Table 4. The principles are informative, but they SHOULD be used to resolve questions of interpretation that the normative text does not settle.

          Table 4: Design principles
          PrincipleStatement
          Catalogue firstThe primary unit is a governed pathway definition that can be discovered, compared, versioned and reused in a catalogue.
          Technology neutralityThe pathway definition is independent of workflow engine, EPR, modelling tool, supplier platform and exchange technology. Local organisational and technology choices are linked through implementation descriptions rather than embedded in the definition.
          Business meaning firstPathways are described in terms recognisable to clinicians, managers and architects responsible for care and service design.
          Human and machine readabilityThe same underlying description supports understandable catalogue views and structured processing.
          Minimum sufficient structureEntities, attributes and requirements are included only where they have demonstrable value for describing, comparing or governing pathways.
          Explicit relationshipsContainment, inclusion, behaviour, responsibility, information dependency, evidence and outcomes are represented explicitly, so that activities and decisions can be traced to evidence, roles, information, implementations and measures.
          Semantic opennessImportant concepts can be bound to recognised terminologies and information standards without requiring one universal terminology or information model.
          Modularity and reuseStages, activities, decisions and sub-pathways are reusable and are referenced rather than duplicated.
          Variation without fragmentationSpecialised pathways retain parent identity and record deviations rather than becoming unrelated copies.
          Lifecycle governancePublished pathway definitions are immutable versions with traceable identity, origin, ownership, approval, authority and change history.
          Progressive formalityGreater computability is available where it is needed but is not inherently better. Formal representations add precision without becoming the source of pathway meaning.

          6.3 Level of detail

          The depth of decomposition SHOULD be determined by the business purpose of the catalogue. Activities are not decomposed into tasks; where finer structure is needed, an activity SHOULD be represented as a stage containing activities, or as an included sub-pathway. A pathway catalogue SHOULD remain usable without exposing the internal configuration detail of execution systems.

          8.8 PathwayStage

          A PathwayStage is a clinically or operationally meaningful subdivision used to organise pathway content. Stages MAY be nested. Care settings are related to stages by occursIn relationships.

A nested stage follows its own ordering once it is available.

          A stage is complete when its completion conditions hold. Where an ordered stage has no completion condition, it is complete when each of its terminal nodes that is not an optional activity has completed or has not been selected by any decision on the route taken; a terminal node is a directly contained node with no outgoing behavioural relationship to another element directly contained in the same stage. An unordered stage SHALL state at least one completion condition. Optional activities do not affect whether a stage is complete. These rules describe intended behaviour for analysis and presentation; they do not define run-time behaviour (9.8). CONF-HPDS-027

          8.10 FormalRepresentation

          A FormalRepresentation identifies a model of all or part of a pathway definition expressed in an external formalism, such as a BPMN process, a CMMN case, a DMN decision model, a FHIR PlanDefinition, a CQL library or WHO DAK content.           8.10.1 Classes of difference

          A difference between a formal representation and the pathway definition it represents is one of the following:

          1. contradiction: the representation asserts meaning that is incompatible with the definition for an element it represents, such as a different population, criterion, decision outcome, ordering, responsibility or intended outcome;
          2. elaboration: the representation adds detail that this document does not represent, such as decomposition of an activity, user-interface steps or data mappings, without contradicting the definition;
          3. incomplete coverage: the representation does not represent some elements of the definition, as declared by representsElement;
          4. unresolved discrepancy: a difference whose classification as contradiction or elaboration has not yet been determined.

          Elaboration and incomplete coverage are permitted and are not defects; they SHOULD be explained in the note component. Contradictions and unresolved discrepancies SHALL be recorded as RepresentationDiscrepancy entries (8.26.11). The correspondence component records only the declared relationship of derivation (derived, source or aligned); it is not used to record differences. CONF-HPDS-031

          8.10.2 Resolving discrepancies

          The approved meaning of a pathway is that of the pathway definition, subject to its authority status and intended use, whatever the declared correspondence. A contradiction is resolved by issuing a new version of the definition, a new version of the representation, or both, and recording the resolution in the discrepancy entry.

          Where correspondence is source, a contradiction indicates an error in the derivation of the definition or a later change to the representation. It SHALL NOT be resolved by treating the representation as authoritative; the owner of the definition SHALL decide which meaning is intended and issue a new version accordingly. Where correspondence is derived, a contradiction is presumed to be a defect in the representation. Where correspondence is aligned, neither is presumed defective until the discrepancy is resolved. CONF-HPDS-032

          8.10.3 Reliance while a discrepancy is open

          While a discrepancy of type contradiction or unresolved has status open, for each affected element:

          1. a consumer MAY rely on the pathway definition, to the extent permitted by its authority status and intended use;
          2. a consumer SHALL NOT rely on the formal representation; CONF-HPDS-033
          3. a presentation of the formal representation, or of the affected element, SHALL indicate that an open discrepancy exists. CONF-HPDS-034

          A consumer MAY rely on elaborations that do not contradict the definition, to the extent permitted by the governance of the representation. Open discrepancies are reported by V66.

          Repetition SHALL be represented only by iterates relationships, each carrying an IterationSpec with a termination condition. The source of an iterates relationship is the last element of the repeated route and its target is the first. CONF-HPDS-048

          9.6 Behavioural reachability

          Reachability is defined over the elements of one pathway definition. The set of reachable elements is the smallest set that satisfies the following rules:

          1. the target of every startsAt relationship is reachable;
          2. where the source of a precedes, selects, branchTo, rejoins, triggers or escalatesTo relationship is reachable, or is an Event whose external attribute is true, its target is reachable if the target is an element of the definition;
          3. where an ordered stage is reachable, its initial nodes (3.60) are reachable;
          4. where an unordered stage is reachable, every node it directly contains is reachable.

          In applying rule c), an incoming relationship counts against a node's being initial only if its source is directly contained in the same stage, or is a PathwayBranch selected by a DecisionPoint directly contained in the same stage. Nested stages are reachable on the same terms as other nodes and then make their contents reachable by rules c) and d). Optional activities are reachable on the same terms as other activities; being optional affects completion only (8.8). An Activity that includes a sub-pathway is treated as a single node (9.3). Structural containment, other than as stated in rules c) and d), and iterates, interrupts, waitsFor, dependency, responsibility, context, outcome and derivation relationships do not make any element reachable.

          At Level 2 and above:

          1. every behavioural node other than an Event whose external attribute is true SHALL be reachable (V28); CONF-HPDS-050
          2. for each EntryPoint, applying the rules above with only the startsAt relationship of that entry point in rule a), at least one ExitPoint SHALL be reachable (V29); CONF-HPDS-051
          3. the target of every waitsFor relationship SHALL be an Event whose external attribute is true or a reachable Event (V67). CONF-HPDS-052

          An Event whose external attribute is true and which has no outgoing behavioural relationship, such as an event used only as a temporal anchor, takes no part in reachability. Reachability is a structural property of the description; it does not indicate that an element is performed for every subject of care.

          9.7 Process execution

          The behavioural graph expresses intended business behaviour with the descriptive semantics stated in 9.8. It is not an executable workflow definition. A BPMN, CMMN, DMN, FHIR or other computable model MAY be referenced from a pathway definition as a formal representation (8.10), or linked to a pathway implementation as an ImplementationArtefact (8.28.2). Execution semantics (1.2) are outside the scope of this document, and no conformance level of this document implies that a pathway can be executed.

          11. Semantic binding

          11.1 General

          This document does not define a clinical terminology. It enables binding to external semantic resources, such as SNOMED CT [SNOMED-CT], ICD, national data dictionaries and information standards, without requiring any one of them.

          11.2 Binding requirements

          A terminology binding SHALL conform to 8.26.1 and SHALL identify the semantic resource, its version and the code, value set or data element identifier. Value set bindings SHALL state their binding strength. CONF-HPDS-057

          11.3 Local terms

          Local terms SHALL be identified by setting localTerm to true and SHALL NOT be represented as codes from an external semantic resource. At Level 3 and above, the clinical concepts on which population inclusion and exclusion criteria and entry and exit criteria depend SHALL be identified by the concept component of their Expressions (8.26.2), with at least one concept for each criterion.           A change to the version of a bound semantic resource SHALL be recorded in the change record of the version that introduces it. Producers SHOULD report bindings to versions that the publisher has withdrawn or superseded, where that information is available. CONF-HPDS-059

          11.5 Binding report

          A Level 3 or Level 4 conformance claim SHALL include a binding report. CONF-HPDS-060

          12. Interoperability requirements

          An InteroperabilityRequirement (8.25) states a business information exchange on which a pathway depends.           13. Specialisation, variation and profiles

          13.1 Specialisation

          A pathway definition MAY specialise another pathway definition, and specialisation MAY be repeated: a national or professional reference pathway MAY be specialised regionally and organisationally before being realised in one or more implementations. A pathway variant SHALL reference its parent and parent version through the parent attribute and a specialises relationship. CONF-HPDS-062

          13.2 Inheritance and deviation

          An inherited element that retains the same meaning SHALL retain its parent identifier. A new element, or an inherited element whose meaning changes materially, SHALL receive a new identifier. Every added, omitted, modified, substituted or reordered element SHALL be recorded as a Deviation (8.26.8) with its rationale and, where applicable, approval and evidence. In a pathway variant, deviations are recorded in the deviation attribute of the HealthcarePathway (8.4); their parentElement references SHALL be qualified by the parent version. CONF-HPDS-063

          At Level 2 and above, the deviations of a variant are complete when, comparing the elements of the variant with those of the referenced parent version by identifier (excluding the HealthcarePathway element itself):

          1. every identifier present in the parent but not in the variant is the parentElement of exactly one Deviation of type omitted or substituted;
          2. every identifier present in the variant but not in the parent is the localElement of exactly one Deviation of type added or substituted;
          3. every identifier present in both whose elements differ in any attribute other than provenance is the parentElement of exactly one Deviation of type modified or reordered, whose changedAttribute lists the differing attributes.

          In this comparison, a reference to the HealthcarePathway element of the parent and a reference to the HealthcarePathway element of the variant are treated as equal.

          Relationship elements are compared in the same way. Completeness is validated by V61. The same recording requirement applies to a PathwayImplementation in relation to the definition it realises, in its deviation attribute (8.27), including a change to the permitted outcomes of a decision point (8.11); because implementations add local elements of other kinds, completeness of implementation deviations is reviewed rather than computed (V47).

          A domain profile MAY tighten cardinalities and obligations, restrict value sets, require particular bindings and define extensions. A description that conforms to a domain profile SHALL also conform to this document. CONF-HPDS-064

          Note

          13.6 Extensions

          14. Governance, provenance and lifecycle

          14.1 Governance metadata

          Every pathway definition and pathway implementation SHALL record owner, version, lifecycle status, authority status and intended use. From the published state onward, effective and review dates SHALL also be recorded; the effective date is content and the review date is a governance-state attribute (14.2). Approvals SHALL be recorded as ApprovalRecords (8.26.6), and clinical authority as clinicalAuthority, in the content or in governance events, so that the requirements of 16.1 are met for the current authority status. A pathway definition published for use outside the owning organisation SHOULD record the rights under which it MAY be reused and specialised. CONF-HPDS-072

          14.2 Content immutability and governance state

          The attributes of a pathway description are divided into content and governance state. The governance-state attributes are lifecycleStatus, authorityStatus, reviewDate and governanceEvent of the HealthcarePathway or PathwayImplementation, and discrepancy of each FormalRepresentation. All other attributes of all elements are content.

          Once a pathway description has been published, its content SHALL NOT be modified; any change to content SHALL produce a new version (Chapter 15). Its governance state MAY change after publication, but only by appending a GovernanceEvent (8.26.10) that records the change or, for representation discrepancies, by adding a RepresentationDiscrepancy or changing the status of an existing one to resolved. Existing governance events and discrepancy records SHALL NOT be removed, and governance events SHALL NOT be altered. CONF-HPDS-073

          A GovernanceEvent of type publication SHALL be recorded when a description is published; it establishes the lifecycle status, authority status and review date at publication. Governance events SHALL be recorded in non-decreasing date order. Each of lifecycleStatus, authorityStatus and reviewDate SHALL equal the value established by the latest governance event that sets it (V62). CONF-HPDS-074

          Historical versions SHALL remain interpretable and SHALL remain retrievable while they are referenced by implementations, catalogues or other pathway definitions. Catalogue manifests have no governance-state attributes; a published catalogue manifest version is wholly immutable. CONF-HPDS-075

          14.3 Provenance

          Provenance SHALL be recorded for the pathway description as a whole and for individual elements whose origin differs materially from that of the whole description. Content imported from a tabular catalogue SHALL carry a ProvenanceRecord with origin imported-from-catalogue. Content derived from a formal representation SHALL carry a ProvenanceRecord with origin derived-from-representation. CONF-HPDS-076

          14.4 Change history

          Every version after the first published version SHALL carry a ChangeRecord (8.26.7) that states the version, date, author, change category, summary and rationale, identifies affected elements where applicable, and records any change of bound resource version. CONF-HPDS-077

          15. Identification and versioning

          15.1 Identifiers

          H.1 Method

          Existing pathway catalogues can be imported by classifying each field as an entity, an attribute, a relationship, governance or provenance information, implementation-specific information, or a compound field that requires decomposition. The mapping should:

          K.3 Success criteria

          The development is to be judged successful if:

          1. substantially different pathways, including sequential and adaptive pathways, can be represented without specialty-specific redesign;
          2. existing catalogues can be migrated without material information loss;
          3. clinicians and managers can understand the human-readable catalogue record;
          4. architects can relate pathways to services, capabilities, information dependencies and implementations without embedding technology choices in pathway definitions;
          5. variants and local implementations can be compared reliably;
          6. machine processing does not depend on uncontrolled narrative for structural meaning;
          7. pathway definitions remain stable business artefacts as technology implementations and modelling notations change.
