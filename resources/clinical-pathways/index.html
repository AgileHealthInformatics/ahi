About the collection

A catalogue-oriented view of clinical pathways.

The resources treat pathway definitions as technology-neutral business artefacts rather than as EPR workflows or patient-level records. They are intended to support analysis, service design, architecture and catalogue development.

Reference material

These resources are not clinical guidance. The pathway definitions are draft and unapproved. Their purpose is to make pathway scope, decisions, roles, information requirements, evidence and relationships inspectable.

Across the collection, pathway definitions remain distinct from local implementations and from the actual care experienced by an individual patient. That separation allows organisations to compare intended models of care without assuming that every organisation must use the same systems, workflow engine or local operating model.

DefinitionThe intended model of care and its business meaning.
ImplementationHow a particular organisation realises that definition through services, workforce and technology.
InstanceThe actual course of care experienced by an individual, which sits outside these reference catalogues.

How to use the collection

Use the navigators to investigate pathways, not to prescribe care.

Service design

Inspect boundaries, hand-offs, roles, decisions, outcomes and dependencies before translating a pathway into local operating arrangements.

Architecture and informatics

Relate information requirements and interoperability concerns to the pathway without embedding the pathway definition in a particular application architecture.

Catalogue development

Use the examples to test whether HPDS can support discoverable, comparable and governable pathway catalogues across different clinical domains.
